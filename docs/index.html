Kidney swaps across hospitals, with no patient data shared.

Many patients waiting for a kidney already have a loved one willing to donate, who is the wrong match. KidneyGrid gives every hospital a Flower agent that keeps its records private, and together the agents find swap loops no hospital could find alone.

The match exists. It's just at another hospital.

Paired kidney exchange lets incompatible donor–patient couples swap donors. But the couples are spread across hospitals that can't pool patient records, so most swaps are never found.

An agent in every hospital

Each hospital runs a KidneyGrid agent on its own Flower SuperNode, next to its data. A coordinator agent never sees a patient record: it works only from anonymous tokens and yes/no answers.

Step 1

Anonymous tokens

Hospitals share donors only as blood and tissue typing tokens, with no names attached.

Step 2

Local checks

Each hospital tests every token against its own patients' antibodies, in code, and answers only yes or no.

Step 3

Best loops, human approval

The coordinator finds the best swap loops (up to 3-way), surgeons approve, and only then are identities released.

Zero matches alone. Five together.

Five couples at five hospitals, none able to donate to their own partner. First-come matching gives Maria's only compatible donor to a patient who could accept almost anyone. KidneyGrid prioritizes the hardest to match.

Simulated pools follow Saidman et al. (2006) as specified by Roth, Sönmez & Ünver (2007), averaged over 5 pools. Simulated pools are denser than real ones; the gap is the point.

Then the agents make it happen

Every surgery in a loop has to happen on the same day, and the kidneys travel. So hospitals and a courier company coordinate Transplant Day, each keeping its own calendar private.

Proposal

Each hospital checks its own operating rooms and beds and shares only which candidate days fit. The coordinator proposes Tuesday, Oct 6 for Loop A.

Emergency

Valley Health in San Jose loses its operating room to an emergency case. The reason stays at the hospital; only "unavailable" is shared.

Re-plan

The agents move the whole loop to Thursday, Oct 8, because no surgery can happen without the others.

Transport

A courier company's agent, a different kind of organization on the same Flower network, books a cold-chain van for every kidney. It sees hospitals and days, never patients.

Safe, private, human-supervised

Privacy is enforced in code, not in a prompt: the model never sees the private data it could leak. A prompt-injection request for the patient list is simply refused.

Crossed the network

  • Anonymous donor typing tokens
  • Yes/no compatibility answers
  • Approve or decline, with a category
  • Names, only after surgeons approve

Never left the hospital

  • Patient names and records
  • Antibody profiles and sensitization
  • Clinical reasons for declining
  • Anything a prompt injection asks for

Built on Flower

KidneyGrid runs live on Flower's SuperGrid in federation @kdotmahesh/kidneygrid, with six organizations as registered SuperNodes.

Hospitals are SuperNodes

Each reads only its own records from its own disk. Capitol Hospital's node runs on Nebius Serverless AI.

Grid messaging

The coordinator uses Flower's Grid tools, get_nodes, push_messages, pull_messages, and hospitals reply with push_reply_message.

One AgentApp, two roles

The same app runs as coordinator on the SuperLink and as hospital agent on every node, chosen by which Grid tools the runtime exposes.

Endeavor 1.0

Each hospital's agent uses Flower's Endeavor model to write its surgeon's briefing, from non-identifying facts only.
